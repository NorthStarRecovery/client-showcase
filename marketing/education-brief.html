UNC Wilmington: Hurricane Florence | 2018

At UNC Wilmington after Hurricane Florence in 2018, recovery covered 130 buildings. First building turnovers began after four weeks; that milestone does not mean all buildings were complete.

01

The challenge

Hurricane Florence damaged residential and other campus facilities at UNC Wilmington in September 2018. Students were displaced and the academic calendar was disrupted. The recovery required work across a varied building portfolio, including nearly one million square feet of affected residential interiors.

02

The work

NorthStar expanded from an initial building assignment to remediation and emergency reconstruction across 130 buildings. The team deployed 750 recovery workers, temporary power and structural drying resources. A command center supported communication with the university as crews worked across the campus and prepared buildings for phased turnover.

03

The milestone

First building turnovers began after four weeks, with recovery continuing across the campus. The milestone describes the start of handovers to the university, rather than completion of every building in the 130-building scope.

Before the next interruption

Put the academic calendar beside the building list.

Bring facilities, operations and the authorized decision-makers together before an incident. Match priority buildings to the services they support, then discuss the dates and temporary arrangements that would shape recovery.

  1. Priority buildings

    Which classrooms, residences and service spaces need to return first, and what functions depend on them?

  2. Decision contacts

    Who can authorize emergency work, approve changes and keep campus or district leadership informed?

  3. Calendar and alternatives

    Which dates, occupied areas and temporary arrangements will influence access and the order of recovery?
