23days to production restart

Following Hurricane Laura in August 2020, production began at Firestone's Lake Charles polymers plant after a 23-day recovery effort. That milestone describes restart; final project completion is unspecified.

Explore the project

Bring the production sequence into the plan.

Start a working session with the plant manager, engineering, EHS and quality. Use the next operating milestone to identify the spaces and decisions that deserve attention before a loss.

  • Critical spaces

    Which production, storage and loading areas must be available together for the next operating milestone?

  • Operating constraints

    Where will access, utilities or adjacent activity affect restoration and material removal?

  • Restart authority

    Which engineering, equipment and quality contacts approve the handover and production restart?

Bring your plant team and NorthStar together to review critical spaces, access needs and the physical recovery priorities behind your next operating milestone.

100days for demolition and remediation

At the ProLogis / FedEx facility in 2020, demolition and remediation covered two tornado-damaged buildings totaling 1.15 million square feet. The 100-day milestone excludes full rebuilding of those facilities.

Explore the project

Make the site's end condition explicit.

Bring site leadership, EHS and the relevant engineering specialists to the table. Review retained operations, available material information and the condition the owner expects when the agreed work is complete.

  • Retained operations

    Which utilities, access routes and assets must remain available, and who authorizes changes to them?

  • Material information

    What surveys, hazard records and disposal requirements are available for the affected structures and materials?

  • Completion criteria

    What must be removed, restored or left ready for the site's next stage?

Discuss site conditions, retained operations and the required end condition with NorthStar. Establish the appropriate recovery and specialist team for the work ahead.

Nationalportfolio program · 2009 to 2015

NorthStar's historical master service agreement with Jones Lang LaSalle and HSBC supported fire, water and disaster recovery across a national portfolio during 2009 to 2015. It is not a current-contract claim.

Explore the project

Start with the property you know best.

Choose one property for a readiness discussion with its manager and building engineer. Use the building's actual access conditions, tenant responsibilities and approval process to make the conversation specific.

  • Authority and approvals

    Who can authorize emergency work, approve changes and brief ownership on recovery decisions?

  • Access and occupancy

    Which entrances, loading routes, occupied areas and working hours will shape the work?

  • Tenant responsibilities

    Who owns each affected space, and who will keep tenants informed as recovery progresses?

Bring your property manager and building engineer into a NorthStar readiness discussion about access, authorization, tenant responsibilities and the information your building team needs.

100,000+square feet of preparatory work

At North Central Bronx Hospital in 2020, preparation across eight floors supported the wider ICU reconstruction program. The four-week intensive effort describes preparatory work, rather than completion of the clinical expansion.

Explore the project

Bring facility requirements into the first conversation.

Invite facilities, infection prevention and emergency management to review the setting together. Begin with occupied areas, access needs and the work information each team needs to support facility-led decisions.

  • Affected and adjacent

    Which departments and occupied spaces sit beside the proposed work, and what access restrictions apply?

  • Utility and water contacts

    Who reviews building utilities and water-system concerns alongside physical restoration work?

  • Facility handoff

    What records and completion reviews do the authorized facility teams need before deciding how space returns to use?

Meet with NorthStar and your designated facility teams to review work areas, access, communication and the information needed for a planned recovery handoff.

130buildings in the recovery scope

At UNC Wilmington after Hurricane Florence in 2018, recovery covered 130 buildings. First building turnovers began after four weeks; that milestone does not mean all buildings were complete.

Explore the project

Put the academic calendar beside the building list.

Bring facilities, operations and the authorized decision-makers together before an incident. Match priority buildings to the services they support, then discuss the dates and temporary arrangements that would shape recovery.

  • Priority buildings

    Which classrooms, residences and service spaces need to return first, and what functions depend on them?

  • Decision contacts

    Who can authorize emergency work, approve changes and keep campus or district leadership informed?

  • Calendar and alternatives

    Which dates, occupied areas and temporary arrangements will influence access and the order of recovery?

Bring your priority building list to NorthStar. Discuss the calendar, essential services and authorized contacts that should guide physical recovery across your campus or district.

Phasedwork around active building services

Pentagon renovations during 2001 to 2011 demonstrate adjacent government-facility experience in phased abatement and demolition around active services. This example does not establish direct data-center project experience for NorthStar.

Explore the project

Bring technical owners into the property plan.

Begin with facilities, EHS and the specialists responsible for critical systems. Review the physical recovery assignment alongside access controls, environmental requirements and the approvals needed before each technical handoff.

  • Critical spaces

    Which rooms and supporting areas need particular access controls or environmental conditions?

  • Authorized work

    Who approves entry, work boundaries and activity near sensitive systems?

  • Technical handoffs

    Which specialists direct isolation, testing, restart and acceptance, and what project information do they need?

Bring your facilities and technical owners into a NorthStar discussion about site requirements, authorized access and the physical recovery work appropriate for your property.
