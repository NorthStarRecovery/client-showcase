Qualifications / Relevant capability

Resolve the decisions that shape the sequence.

A decommissioning plan becomes more useful when ownership is clear. Start with the decisions below, then connect them to the required technical work and specialist input.

Choose asset destinations

Identify equipment to retain, relocate or recover before defining removal methods. Establish ownership, condition records, handling requirements and the destination for each priority asset.

Map utility dependencies

Identify active and retired systems, shared services and the parties authorized to approve isolation. Confirm dependencies with retained buildings or neighboring operations.

Bring conditions forward

Share hazardous-material surveys and environmental information early. Connect required abatement or remediation with the dismantlement sequence and material-management plan.

Set the end condition

Define what remains, what is removed and how completion will be accepted. Include restoration and documentation requirements rather than treating them as separate closing questions.

Owner conversation / Define the work

Bring these four inputs to the first discussion.

The initial conversation does not require every answer. It does benefit from a clear picture of what is known, what is changing and who can decide.

  • Facility and asset records

    Assemble available drawings, equipment inventories, maintenance information and condition records. Flag assets with relocation, resale or reuse priorities.

  • Operating and utility constraints

    Identify continuing operations, shared services, access limitations and potential shutdown windows. Name the parties responsible for utility and operating authorizations.

  • Environmental information

    Provide material surveys, investigations and known residual conditions. Identify consultants and the requirements that may govern handling, remediation or disposition.

  • Turnover definition

    Describe the next owner, use or construction phase. Specify retained infrastructure, restoration expectations, required records and the people who will accept completion.

A nuclear facility requires its own specialist decommissioning team and regulatory scope. NorthStar's broader capability set supports that separate conversation; a conventional facility plan is not a substitute.
